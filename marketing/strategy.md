# META_THEORY — marketing strategy

A plan for getting the kit in front of the people it actually helps, without
overselling a small, honest tool. Companion to [`copy.md`](./copy.md).

---

## 1. Positioning

**One sentence:**
> META_THEORY is a subject-agnostic kit of prompts and Claude Code skills that
> forces you to map a craft's foundation *before* you build above it with an AI.

**What it is:** a discipline, packaged. Six prompts, a few skills, one spec.

**What it is not:** a prompt-pack for generating output, an AI course, a SaaS, or a
framework. It produces no artifact except clarity about *what to learn in what
order*. Naming that honestly is the positioning — it sits in a category most prompt
collections don't even acknowledge exists.

---

## 2. The problem (and who feels it)

The trap is **sequence, not ignorance**. An LLM answers at the altitude you ask, so
it hands you results without the foundation underneath them. You accumulate
sophistication on a base you never wrote down; the gap is invisible until a
late-stage decision exposes it.

People who feel this acutely:

- Builders shipping ambitious projects fast with AI assistants, who sense they're
  "flying blind above something."
- Generalists working outside their home domain (a coder doing audio, a designer
  doing ML) where they don't know what they don't know.
- Anyone who has had the specific gut-punch of discovering a foundational concept
  *after* building on top of where it should have gone.

If a reader has never felt that, no amount of copy will land. The strategy is to
**find the people already nodding**, not to convince the unbothered.

---

## 3. Audience segments

| Segment | Why they care | Where they are |
|---|---|---|
| **Primary — AI-assisted builders** | Live inside the altitude trap daily; Claude Code skills are directly usable | r/ClaudeAI, Hacker News, X dev circles, Claude Code community |
| **Secondary — prompt-craft & meta-learning folks** | Drawn to the *method*, will remix the prompts | r/PromptEngineering, learning-in-public communities, Dev.to |
| **Tertiary — creative technologists / indie makers** | Cross-domain work makes the foundation gap vivid | Bluesky, Mastodon, niche Discords, gamedev/music-tool forums |

**Not the audience:** people wanting one-shot output prompts, "10x your
productivity" seekers, anyone allergic to a deliberate slow-down step. Don't chase
them; the message reads as friction to them, and that's correct.

---

## 4. Message pillars

Every piece should ladder back to one of these:

1. **Altitude.** A model answers at the altitude you ask. (The mechanism.)
2. **Sequence beats knowledge.** The order you learn things in *is* the expertise.
3. **One reflex fixes it.** Ask for the map up front; re-check on a cadence.
4. **Any domain.** Code, music, design, writing — anything with deep roots.

Keep pillar 1 in nearly everything; it's the memorable, arguable hook.

---

## 5. Differentiation

- **vs. prompt libraries:** they optimize the *answer*; this optimizes the
  *question's altitude* and *learning order*. Different axis entirely.
- **vs. "AI literacy" threads:** those are tips; this is a repeatable, runnable
  procedure with skills that automate the recurring part.
- **vs. doing nothing:** the cost of skipping it is deferred and invisible, which
  is exactly why a packaged, low-friction reminder has value.

The defensible edge is the **altitude framing** plus **automation** (skills on a
loop, the maestro portfolio-auditor concept). Most people stop at "good prompts."

---

## 6. Channels, ranked

1. **Hacker News (Show HN).** Best fit: technical, method-curious, rewards honest
   framing of a real problem. Highest upside, one shot — launch when the README and
   prompts are tight. Lead with the problem, not the repo.
2. **r/ClaudeAI.** Directly relevant audience; the skills are immediately usable.
   Post the problem, link in comments.
3. **X/Twitter.** The altitude one-liner is built to travel. Thread on launch,
   then steady single posts pulling one idea each from the prompts.
4. **Dev.to / personal blog.** Long-form home for the full argument; the canonical
   piece everything else links back to.
5. **Bluesky / Mastodon.** Lower volume, higher signal; good for the meta-learning
   and creative-tech crowds.
6. **Product Hunt.** Optional, low priority — the kit isn't a product launch, and
   PH rewards polish/novelty over method. Only if a quiet day needs filling.

---

## 7. Launch sequence

**Phase 0 — Ready the asset.** README stands on its own, prompts are clean and
copy-pasteable, banner reads at a glance, repo public, LICENSE chosen. The repo is
the landing page; nothing ships until it's self-explanatory to a cold reader.

**Phase 1 — Seed quietly.** Share in one or two communities where you already have
standing. Collect feedback on the *prompts* specifically. Fix friction.

**Phase 2 — Canonical post.** Publish the long-form blog piece (the argument, not
the feature list). This is the link every later post points at.

**Phase 3 — Show HN + Reddit.** Launch on the strongest channels the same week,
not the same hour. Be present for comments — the discussion is the marketing.

**Phase 4 — Steady drip.** One idea per post, mined from the six prompts, on a
sustainable cadence. The kit is evergreen; treat distribution as a slow loop, not a
spike. (Fitting, given the product.)

---

## 8. Content engine

The kit is its own content backlog. Each prompt is a post:

- "Map the ladder before you climb it" — the cartographer prompt as a walkthrough.
- "How to find the canon of any craft in 20 minutes" — canon-finder.
- "Trace the roots: why every modern rule used to be an older rule" — roots.
- "Run a pre-mortem on your project's foundations" — pre-mortem.
- "Put your self-audit on a loop" — altitude-check + the loop skill.

Each shows the prompt, a real run, and the output. Demonstration beats assertion.

---

## 9. Success metrics

Honest, small-project metrics — not vanity:

- **Resonance:** comments and replies that say *"this is exactly my problem,"* not
  raw upvotes. One of those is worth fifty stars.
- **Use:** stars and forks as a weak proxy; better, anyone reporting they *ran* a
  prompt and it changed a decision.
- **Remix:** people adapting the prompts to their own domain — the clearest signal
  the method generalized.
- **Inbound:** the kit functioning as a credibility artifact (it demonstrates taste
  and meta-level thinking) more than as a growth funnel.

Set no follower or star target. The goal is reaching the people who feel the
problem, and being useful to them.

---

## 10. Risks & honest caveats

- **"Sounds profound, does little."** The mitigation is always showing a concrete
  run with real output. Demonstrate, never just assert.
- **Abstract-on-arrival.** The altitude idea is sticky but the value is felt only
  after use. Lower the activation cost: make the very first prompt trivially
  copy-pasteable with an obvious payoff.
- **Narrow channel risk.** Heavily Claude-Code-flavored framing can read as niche.
  Counter it by leading with the universal problem (sequence/altitude) and treating
  the skills as the power-user layer, not the headline.
- **Over-marketing a small tool.** The fastest way to lose this audience is hype.
  Restraint *is* the strategy; the voice has to match the substance.
