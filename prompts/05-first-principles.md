# 05 · The First-Principles Dig

> The deepest invariants — *why* the techniques work, the rules that would survive a
> change of genre, medium, or technology.

A zoom-in on the bottom of the [Cartographer's](./01-cartographer.md) map. This is
the prompt that produced **Part I (the philosophy)** of this repo's
[`song_construction_basics.md`](../../research/song_construction_basics.md) —
tension/release, expectation/surprise, unity/variety, and so on. It converts a pile
of recipes into a small set of reasons.

- **Fire when:** after mapping, when you want the principles that *generate* the
  techniques rather than memorizing the techniques themselves.
- **Engine:** research + reasoning — `deep-research` skill, or direct for a craft
  you already know well.
- **Pairs with:** [`04-roots`](./04-roots.md) (the historical complement); its
  output is the richest seed for a real foundation doc.

## The prompt

```
Beneath the techniques and recipes of <DOMAIN>, what are the deepest
invariant principles — the ones that explain WHY the techniques work, that
would survive even if the genre/medium/technology changed completely? Tie
each principle back to a concrete technique it justifies, and to a known
failure mode it prevents.
```

## Variables

- `<DOMAIN>` — the craft. (Add "for a builder/generator, not a consumer" if you
  want codifiable rules rather than appreciation.)

## What good output looks like

- A *small* set of principles (≈5–7), each phrased as an invariant, not a tip.
- Each tied **down** to a concrete technique it justifies and **up** to a failure
  mode (e.g. "no planned arc → wandering").
- Principles that are medium-agnostic — they should read as true even if you swap
  the technology out.

## The failure it prevents

Cargo-culting techniques you can't reason about, and being unable to improvise when
the situation falls outside your recipe set. Principles let you *derive* a new move
instead of looking one up.
