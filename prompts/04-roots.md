# 04 · The Roots

> Trace the long, twisted lineage — where the craft came from, and which inherited
> principles still silently govern it.

A zoom-in on the *historical/intellectual* dimension of the
[Cartographer's](./01-cartographer.md) map. Crafts with deep roots borrowed their
deepest rules from older disciplines; surfacing the root makes a modern rule feel
*inevitable* instead of arbitrary — and often hands you a principle you can
transplant.

- **Fire when:** after you have the map, when a craft's rules feel arbitrary, or
  when you want the "majesty" — the deep why-it's-shaped-this-way story.
- **Engine:** research — `deep-research` skill.
- **Pairs with:** [`05-first-principles`](./05-first-principles.md) (roots explain
  *where* the invariants came from; first-principles names *what* they are).

## The prompt

```
Trace the deep lineage of <DOMAIN>. What older disciplines feed it, and
what principles did it inherit from them? (E.g. music borrows from
rhetoric, poetry, math, dance; games from theater, sport, ritual,
simulation; storytelling from oral tradition, myth, drama.) Which of those
borrowed principles still silently govern the modern craft — and would
understanding the root make the modern rules feel inevitable instead of
arbitrary?
```

## Variables

- `<DOMAIN>` — the craft, plus optionally an era or tradition to anchor the
  lineage.

## What good output looks like

- A genealogy: the **parent disciplines** and *what each contributed*.
- For each inherited principle: the modern rule it still governs, made to feel
  inevitable ("phrasing is breath because the form descends from sung poetry").
- At least one **transplantable principle** — something true in the root that you
  can reapply deliberately in the modern work.

## The failure it prevents

Treating a craft's conventions as a flat bag of tricks. Roots reveal *which*
conventions are deep invariants (keep, lean on) versus shallow fashion (break
freely) — and that distinction is most of taste.
